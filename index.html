ISW imprint of a void

In ΛCDM, dark energy makes a void’s potential hill flatten while CMB photons cross it. They lose more energy climbing in than they regain leaving, so the void appears as a cold spot. AvERA has no dark energy, but its uneven expansion has a similar effect, and above z ≈ 1.8 voids even leave hot spots.

How it’s computed

The void has the compensated profile δ(r) = δc(1 − 2r²/3r0²)e−r²/r0², empty in the middle and ringed by a denser wall. Its radius Rv is where δ crosses zero, Rv = √1.5 r0. The Poisson equation gives a Gaussian potential Φ ∝ (D/a) e−r²/r0².

Integrating ΔT/T = (2/c²)∫ Φ̇ dτ through the void (linear theory, thin-lens approximation) gives

ΔT/T = (√π/2) (H0r0/c)² Ωm (H r0/c) δc (1 − f) e−θ²/θ0²

where H(z) is the expansion rate, f = d ln D/d ln a the growth rate of structure and θ0 = r0/χ(z). If f = 1 the potential is frozen and there is no imprint; f < 1 gives a cold spot for a void, f > 1 a hot spot. δc is the void’s contrast at its own redshift, the same in both models, and radii use h = 0.6774 in both.

ΛCDM: flat, H0 = 67.74, Ωm = 0.3089 (Planck 2015, the cosmology AvERA starts from at z ≈ 9).

AvERA: H(z) and Ωm,eff(z) come from the simulation logfiles published with Pataki et al. (2025), runs with 1080k particles (used by Beck et al. 2018) and 625k (their baseline). The growth rate f(z) comes from solving the linear growth equation D̈ + 2HḊ = 4πGρ̄D in that expansion history. This gives a sign flip above z ≈ 1.9 (1080k) or 1.7 (625k). Beck et al. report it for z ≈ 1.5–4.4; their growth factor was computed differently, so the limits differ somewhat.

The background is a Gaussian random field drawn from an approximate Planck temperature power spectrum on a flat 72° × 36° patch.
